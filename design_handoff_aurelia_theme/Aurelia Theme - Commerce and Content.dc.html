Why we engrave by hand, and why it takes ten days

19 August 2026 · by Túlio Menezes, bench jeweller

A machine cuts identical letters, at identical depth, with a matte floor. It is fast and it is correct, and it is the reason most engraving looks like a label rather than part of the ring.

A hand graver pushes metal aside instead of removing it. The wall of the cut stays mirror-bright, so the letter flashes as your hand turns and then disappears. That is what people mean when they say old jewellery looks alive.

“You are not cutting the letter. You are cutting the light that will sit inside it.”

The ten days, accounted for

Three days for the band to be cast, cleaned and pre-polished. One to lay out the letters in wax pencil and get your approval by photo. One to cut. Two to re-finish the face around the cut without softening its edges. Three in the queue, because there is one bench and one pair of hands.

  1. Up to three characters, in our house serif or your own handwriting.
  2. Engraved pieces can still be returned within 30 days.
  3. Re-cutting after a resize is free, forever.

House policy

Care & repair

Everything we make is repaired free, for as long as you own it. That is not a warranty against damage — it is the opposite. It assumes damage, because a piece worn daily will be knocked, bent and worn thin.

Resizing

Up to three sizes in either direction, as often as your hands change. We re-cut any engraving at the same time. Post it to us and we cover the return.

Polishing

We polish conservatively — enough to bring the surface back, never enough to soften an edge. Say the word and we will leave the patina alone entirely.
