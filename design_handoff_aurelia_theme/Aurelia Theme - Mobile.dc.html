Why we engrave by hand, and why it takes ten days

19 August 2026 · Túlio Menezes

A machine cuts identical letters, at identical depth, with a matte floor. It is fast and correct, and it is why most engraving looks like a label rather than part of the ring.

A hand graver pushes metal aside instead of removing it. The wall of the cut stays mirror-bright, so the letter flashes as your hand turns and then disappears.

“You are not cutting the letter. You are cutting the light that will sit inside it.”

The ten days, accounted for

Three days to cast, clean and pre-polish. One to lay out the letters and get your approval by photo. One to cut. Two to re-finish the face. Three in the queue, because there is one bench.
